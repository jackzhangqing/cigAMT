01 / THE BENCHMARK

Overview

Data and evaluation for the coupled electric and magnetic observations used in subsurface exploration.

Abstract

Audio-magnetotelluric (AMT) sensing uses natural electromagnetic fields to probe subsurface electrical structure, supporting mineral exploration, groundwater assessment, and geothermal exploration. Reliable AMT interpretation requires identifying informative lightning-generated transients, classifying interference types and localizing contaminated intervals, and restoring corrupted or incomplete multicomponent recordings. These tasks are complicated by low signal-to-noise ratios, diverse interference patterns, and missing observations, while restoration must preserve the electric–magnetic amplitude and phase relationships needed for reliable impedance estimation. Deep learning offers a promising approach to these challenges by capturing complex temporal patterns and cross-component dependencies, but the lack of large-scale, openly available annotated AMT datasets and unified benchmarks hinders the systematic development and reproducible evaluation of learning-based methods. We introduce cigAMT, a massive-scale, multi-rate benchmark for AMT signal understanding and restoration. Combining curated field measurements, physics-based synthesis, and generative augmentation, cigAMT provides 500,045 four-component reference windows at 150 Hz, a separate library of 718,359 noise segments, and 16,878 annotated windows at 24 kHz. It supports eight tasks: sferic identification, noise classification and localization, time-domain and frequency-component denoising, forecasting, interpolation, and imputation. We benchmark classical and learning-based methods under controlled noise and missing-data conditions, complementing signal-level metrics with impedance-estimation case studies. Across both noise-injection protocols, TCN and U-Net1D achieve point-level F1 scores above 98.9% but differ by 5.1 percentage points in event-level F1, revealing distinctions obscured by point-level evaluation. Forecasting remains challenging under the evaluated protocol, with the best baseline attaining a mean Pearson correlation of only 0.219 on held-out future segments. cigAMT provides a common testbed for reproducible comparison and future research on physically faithful learning for multicomponent electromagnetic signals.

How are controlled degradations defined?

Noise injection

Six whole-window SNR settings: −30, −25, −20, −15, −10, and −5 dB. Protocol A calibrates each component separately; Protocol C allocates noise using electric/magnetic group energies.

Missing observations

Forecast horizons: 10, 24, 32, 36, 64, 128, 256 samples. Internal gaps: 10, 32, 64, 128, 256 samples. Imputation covers 10–90% nominal missingness and synchronous or independent channel masks.

Labels and splits

The 24 kHz pool contains 512 source records; 27 are annotated. Original labeled windows are split into 11,260 / 3,130 / 2,488 training / validation / test windows. Fixed augmentation adds 11,260 training entries.
