01 / THE BENCHMARK

From natural fields to reliable signals.

Data and evaluation for the coupled electric and magnetic observations used in subsurface exploration.

Why AMT?

Audio-magnetotelluric (AMT) sensing uses natural electromagnetic fields to probe subsurface electrical structure, with applications in mineral exploration, groundwater assessment, and geothermal exploration. Its measurements contain weak, variable natural signals alongside interference and missing observations.

cigAMT brings curated field measurements, physics-based synthesis, training-only generative augmentation, and separate noise resources into a common benchmark. It evaluates signal identification, interference analysis, denoising, and missing-data recovery, with impedance case studies that connect waveform processing to geophysical interpretation.

How are controlled degradations defined?

Noise injection

Six whole-window SNR settings: −30, −25, −20, −15, −10, and −5 dB. Protocol A calibrates each component separately; Protocol C allocates noise using electric/magnetic group energies.

Missing observations

Forecast horizons: 10, 24, 32, 36, 64, 128, 256 samples. Internal gaps: 10, 32, 64, 128, 256 samples. Imputation covers 10–90% nominal missingness and synchronous or independent channel masks.

Labels and splits

The 24 kHz pool contains 512 source records; 27 are annotated. Original labeled windows are split into 11,260 / 3,130 / 2,488 training / validation / test windows. Fixed augmentation adds 11,260 training entries.

05 / GEOPHYSICAL ASSESSMENT

Beyond waveform error.

Supplementary impedance case studies examine how processing affects apparent resistivity, phase, and electromagnetic component relationships.

Preserving the E–H relationship

AMT interpretation uses the relationship between horizontal electric and magnetic components. Pointwise waveform scores alone provide an incomplete view of downstream response estimates.

  • Inspect apparent resistivity and phase after processing.
  • Compare restored and reference responses where clean references are available.
  • Examine response changes on field records without assuming a known clean target.

Impedance assessment complements sferic identification and the two denoising tasks. It is not a ninth benchmark task.
